## Preamble: rulings of record

1. THE THREE-PART DECOUPLING: project length, depreciation, and repayment are THREE INDEPENDENT PARTS that do not lock each other down. The horizon DERIVES: max end across the cost spans, the operating spans, every contract's last collection, every reinvestment month, the residual month, the financing service end, and the project length when set (a floor, never a cap). PROJ-LENGTH-EXCEEDED IS RETIRED - the walkthrough's collision (project length 3 against schedules to month 228 and a residual at month 360) is resolved by derivation, not by veto; the month-wording item of the walkthrough ledger is SUPERSEDED by this retirement (disclosed).
2. CONTRACTS ARE DEALS, NOT COST CARRIERS (the user, verbatim: "A project costs what a project costs. Then we have deals where we can meet different terms."): NO cost-to-lease allocation, ever. A lease has no standalone IRR (an IRR needs an outflow, and the outflow belongs to the whole project). The app tells the IRR FOR ANY GIVEN PERIOD - the whole project's flow truncated at the boundary: the user's example of record ("Say after first term IRR is negative, second term IRR is 0, third term IRR is reaching the target. That means across the three agreements we reach our target but any one of not enough."). Per-term figures are the WHOLE PROJECT'S POSITION AT EACH CONTRACT'S BOUNDARY: the truncated-flow IRR, the cumulative net, the NPV to date - labeled "Project position at the end of <contract>", never "<contract> IRR".
3. DEPRECIATION IS THE BOOK VIEW ONLY (the user asked whether depreciation is a negative cash flow - it is not): the cash left once, when the investment was paid; the cost vector already records it. Depreciation NEVER enters the cash-flow vectors or any appraisal output - recording it would DOUBLE-COUNT the investment. It owns the yearly book schedule and the SPLIT OF RECORD: the written-down part amortizes, the retained part's ending book value meets the residual. The terminology of record: DEPRECIATION. The granularity is flexible, project to project: each cost line carries its own schedule (straight-line over a set time, or a yearly rate), with a project default for unconfigured lines; "retained" marks a line that never writes down; the schedule resolves to yearly book amounts.
4. THE RESIDUAL IS THE RETAINED PART: a terminal cash inflow at its year (the existing behavior stands - it enters the flow, reduces the solved requirement, and counts in the total collected). The basis extends: a static amount (today) OR A PERCENT OF THE INVESTED COST BASE - the percent applies to the total invested cost INCLUDING reinvestments (the project costs what it costs; disclosed, flaggable at the relay). The user's example: 80 MSEK of a 100 MSEK project. Residual value and remaining book value are the same by design in this model; the book view discloses any gain or loss on sale (residual minus remaining book value at the residual year) rather than assuming it away.
5. BACKWARD COMPATIBILITY OF RECORD: a project with no contracts list computes BYTE-IDENTICALLY to today - the legacy single repayment configuration maps to ONE solved contract. The existing template/demo stays single-contract, so the fixture figures and the standing pins (core 194, web 222) hold. A NEW multi-lease demo project enters as a second fixture exercising the capability.
6. THE RIDING REMEDIATIONS of the walkthrough ledger fold into this pass: the waterfall rebuilt in the true B4 form; the hurdle plot's honest solved-mode disclosure; the version label bumped to v0.6 with the pin re-anchored to bind label = constant = current version; the blank-project default repayment interval ANNUALLY (quarterly stays an option; the demo template flip is absorbed by the new demo fixture decision and does not change the standing fixture).
7. THE APPRAISAL CONVENTIONS STAND: monthly basis, end-of-month discounting, the Mode A identity as the primary invariant (generalized below), bracketed bisection with the ambiguity discipline, MIRR and PI on their stated bases, no rounding of intermediates.
8. REINVESTMENTS ARE REAL CASH (the user: "the ability to add reinvestments, say for adjustments, required in connection with each negotiated deal"): unlike depreciation, a reinvestment is a genuine cash OUTFLOW. It enters the cost vector at its month (the standing CAPEX convention: placed at the end of its year), raises the solved requirement in solved mode, weighs on every boundary position it precedes, and is visible on every surface - never a book-only figure. It belongs to its contract as a deal-linked cost entry, and it joins the invested cost base (the depreciation book schedule and the residual percent basis include it).

## Task A - Contracts as deals (core)

- ModelInputs gains contracts?: ContractParams[] - each contract: id, label, startYear, termYears, paymentsPerYear (1/2/4/12), graceYears, escalationPerYear, balloon, mode ("solved" | "evaluated"), and for evaluated contracts: a per-period payment or a per-year collections profile (the existing profile pattern).
- THE LEGACY MAPPING: contracts absent or empty - the existing repayment parameters behave as ONE solved contract, byte-identical outputs (pinned; the standing battery is the proof).
- THE GENERALIZED SOLVE - ONE SCALAR: every solved contract pays the same per-period amount k (each scaled by its own escalation and timing); k is solved so the whole-project flow hits the target IRR. The Mode A identity generalizes: the requirement = cost NPV + operating NPV + PV(reinvestments) - PV(evaluated collections) - PV(residual) - PV(balloons), and k = requirement / (the summed discounted slots of all solved contracts). The leveraged variant generalizes identically (the equity earns the target).
- VALIDATION (visible, never silent): tariff collection modes and contracts are mutually exclusive (a project runs one or the other); app modes B and C require exactly one solved contract; a solved set with zero payment slots is invalid; the summed balloon PVs must stay below the requirement (the existing single-balloon rule generalizes to the set); contracts may overlap (deals can overlap - no validation against it); terms positive, frequencies in {1, 2, 4, 12}; reinvestment amounts positive and years within the horizon floor logic.
- DIRECTIONAL CLAIMS (script-verify before pinning; the sanity heuristics of record): adding a solved contract lowers k; shifting a solved contract later raises k (deferral discounts its slots); adding an evaluated contract lowers k (it carries part of the requirement); grace up on a solved contract raises k at positive target rates; a larger residual lowers k; ADDING A REINVESTMENT RAISES k (the requirement grows by its PV); a reinvestment placed before a boundary LOWERS that boundary's truncated IRR. Every claim is script-verified at the checkpoint before its pin lands.

## Task A2 - Reinvestments per deal (core)

- Each contract carries reinvestments?: { year: number; amount: number }[] - adjustments required in connection with the negotiated deal (a refurbishment or adjustment when the deal starts, a mid-term upgrade). Each enters the cost vector at the end of its year (the CAPEX convention), joins the invested cost base, and appears in the cost grids as its own row per contract.
- In solved mode the reinvestments raise the requirement (the identity above); in evaluated mode they simply weigh on the flow and every boundary position after them.
- A reinvestment is NOT part of the contract's collections, is NOT a lease cost allocation (ruling 2 stands - the outflow belongs to the whole project; the contract link is provenance, visible on every surface), and is NOT book-only (ruling 8: real cash).
- The depreciation book schedule includes reinvestments as capex from their year, following the project default unless configured; the residual percent basis includes them (ruling 4).

## Task B - The derived horizon (core)

- totalMonths = max(every cost span end, every operating span end, every contract's last collection month, every reinvestment month, the residual month, the financing service end, projectLengthYears x 12 when set). The derivation is returned (the horizon plus its constituents) and displayed with its derivation disclosed.
- PROJ-LENGTH-EXCEEDED retires. Per-item consistency validation remains (a contract with a non-positive term, a residual year below the first collection, a financing service start outside the horizon - visible messages, years in vocabulary, per the display convention).

## Task C - Depreciation as the book view (core)

- CostLine gains an optional depreciation config: mode ("straight-line" | "rate" | "retained"), years, yearlyRatePct; a project-level default applies to unconfigured lines. The engine produces a yearly book schedule per line and combined: beginning book value, charge, ending book value, over the derived horizon.
- The book result view: collections minus operating minus depreciation, per year, labeled the book view and disclosed as NOT cash flow. The gain-or-loss-on-sale line: residual minus remaining book value at the residual year (zero by design when the retained part meets the residual; disclosed either way).
- THE DOUBLE-COUNT BAN (the pin of record): the cash-flow vectors and EVERY appraisal output are BIT-IDENTICAL with or without any depreciation configuration. Red proof: injecting a depreciation charge into the cost vector goes red on the equality pin.

## Task D - Residual bases (core)

- ResidualParams extends: the static amount (today's behavior, unchanged) or a percent-of-invested-cost basis (percentPct of the total invested cost base including reinvestments, at the set year). Both enter once, as the terminal inflow at the residual month, and both reduce the solved requirement - no other touchpoints.
- The percent basis is script-verified at the checkpoint: the terminal inflow equals percentPct x total invested cost, and k falls as the percent rises.

## Task E - Per-term boundary appraisal (core, then web)

- For each contract, at its end month: the whole project's position - the truncated-flow IRR (the ambiguity discipline travels; null when the flow has not yet turned - the user's example of record renders negative after term one, near zero after term two, at the target after term three; across the agreements the target is reached, no single term suffices), the cumulative net (nominal), the NPV at WACC and at target of the truncated flow, and payback-so-far (null when not yet). Reinvestments precede their boundaries and are included in every truncation.
- The derivation is GENERAL (the position at any month); the pinned surface is the contract-boundary set.
- Labeled "Project position at the end of <contract>" on every surface - the label discipline is pinned (a surface claiming a contract's standalone IRR goes red).
- The combined figures (the existing achieved IRR, NPV, payback, DSCR) stand, computed over the derived horizon.
- DSCR stays whole-project and yearly (per-contract DSCR is out of scope, disclosed).
