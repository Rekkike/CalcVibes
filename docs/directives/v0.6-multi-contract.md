## Preamble: rulings of record

1. THE THREE-PART DECOUPLING: project length, depreciation, and repayment are THREE INDEPENDENT PARTS that do not lock each other down. The horizon DERIVES: max end across the cost spans, the operating spans, every contract's last collection, the residual month, the financing service end, and the project length when set (a floor, never a cap). PROJ-LENGTH-EXCEEDED IS RETIRED - the walkthrough's collision (project length 3 against schedules to month 228 and a residual at month 360) is resolved by derivation, not by veto; the month-wording item of the walkthrough ledger is SUPERSEDED by this retirement (disclosed).
2. CONTRACTS ARE DEALS, NOT COST CARRIERS (the user, verbatim: "A project costs what a project costs. Then we have deals where we can meet different terms."): NO cost-to-lease allocation, ever. A lease has no standalone IRR (an IRR needs an outflow, and the outflow belongs to the whole project). Per-term figures are the WHOLE PROJECT'S POSITION AT EACH CONTRACT'S BOUNDARY: the truncated-flow IRR, the cumulative net, the NPV to date - labeled "Project position at the end of <contract>", never "<contract> IRR".
3. DEPRECIATION IS THE BOOK VIEW ONLY (the user asked whether depreciation is a negative cash flow - it is not): the cash left once, when the investment was paid; the cost vector already records it. Depreciation NEVER enters the cash-flow vectors or any appraisal output - recording it would DOUBLE-COUNT the investment. It owns the yearly book schedule and the SPLIT OF RECORD: the written-down part amortizes, the retained part's ending book value meets the residual. The terminology of record: DEPRECIATION. The granularity is flexible, project to project: each cost line carries its own schedule (straight-line over a set time, or a yearly rate), with a project default for unconfigured lines; "retained" marks a line that never writes down; the schedule resolves to yearly book amounts.
4. THE RESIDUAL IS THE RETAINED PART: a terminal cash inflow at its year (the existing behavior stands - it enters the flow, reduces the solved requirement, and counts in the total collected). The basis extends: a static amount (today) OR A PERCENT OF THE COST BASE (the user's example: 80 MSEK of a 100 MSEK project). Residual value and remaining book value are the same by design in this model; the book view discloses any gain or loss on sale (residual minus remaining book value at the residual year) rather than assuming it away.
5. BACKWARD COMPATIBILITY OF RECORD: a project with no contracts list computes BYTE-IDENTICALLY to today - the legacy single repayment configuration maps to ONE solved contract. The existing template/demo stays single-contract, so the fixture figures and the standing pins (core 194, web 222) hold. A NEW multi-lease demo project enters as a second fixture exercising the capability.
6. THE RIDING REMEDIATIONS of the walkthrough ledger fold into this pass: the waterfall rebuilt in the true B4 form; the hurdle plot's honest solved-mode disclosure; the version label bumped to v0.6 with the pin re-anchored to bind label = constant = current version; the blank-project default repayment interval ANNUALLY (quarterly stays an option; the demo template flip is absorbed by the new demo fixture decision and does not change the standing fixture).
7. THE APPRAISAL CONVENTIONS STAND: monthly basis, end-of-month discounting, the Mode A identity as the primary invariant (generalized below), bracketed bisection with the ambiguity discipline, MIRR and PI on their stated bases, no rounding of intermediates.

## Task A - Contracts as deals (core)

- ModelInputs gains contracts?: ContractParams[] - each contract: id, label, startYear, termYears, paymentsPerYear (1/2/4/12), graceYears, escalationPerYear, balloon, mode ("solved" | "evaluated"), and for evaluated contracts: a per-period payment or a per-year collections profile (the existing profile pattern).
- THE LEGACY MAPPING: contracts absent or empty - the existing repayment parameters behave as ONE solved contract, byte-identical outputs (pinned; the standing battery is the proof).
- THE GENERALIZED SOLVE - ONE SCALAR: every solved contract pays the same per-period amount k (each scaled by its own escalation and timing); k is solved so the whole-project flow hits the target IRR. The Mode A identity generalizes: the requirement = cost NPV + operating NPV - PV(evaluated collections) - PV(residual) - PV(balloons), and k = requirement / (the summed discounted slots of all solved contracts). The leveraged variant generalizes identically (the equity earns the target).
- VALIDATION (visible, never silent): tariff collection modes and contracts are mutually exclusive (a project runs one or the other); app modes B and C require exactly one solved contract; a solved set with zero payment slots is invalid; the summed balloon PVs must stay below the requirement (the existing single-balloon rule generalizes to the set); contracts may overlap (deals can overlap - no validation against it); terms positive, frequencies in {1, 2, 4, 12}.
- DIRECTIONAL CLAIMS (script-verify before pinning; the sanity heuristics of record): adding a solved contract lowers k; shifting a solved contract later raises k (deferral discounts its slots); adding an evaluated contract lowers k (it carries part of the requirement); grace up on a solved contract raises k at positive target rates; a larger residual lowers k. Every claim is script-verified at the checkpoint before its pin lands.

## Task B - The derived horizon (core)

- totalMonths = max(every cost span end, every operating span end, every contract's last collection month, the residual month, the financing service end, projectLengthYears x 12 when set). The derivation is returned (the horizon plus its constituents) and displayed with its derivation disclosed.
- PROJ-LENGTH-EXCEEDED retires. Per-item consistency validation remains (a contract with a non-positive term, a residual year below the first collection, a financing service start outside the horizon - visible messages, years in vocabulary, per the display convention).

## Task C - Depreciation as the book view (core)

- CostLine gains an optional depreciation config: mode ("straight-line" | "rate" | "retained"), years, yearlyRatePct; a project-level default applies to unconfigured lines. The engine produces a yearly book schedule per line and combined: beginning book value, charge, ending book value, over the derived horizon.
- The book result view: collections minus operating minus depreciation, per year, labeled the book view and disclosed as NOT cash flow. The gain-or-loss-on-sale line: residual minus remaining book value at the residual year (zero by design when the retained part meets the residual; disclosed either way).
- THE DOUBLE-COUNT BAN (the pin of record): the cash-flow vectors and EVERY appraisal output are BIT-IDENTICAL with or without any depreciation configuration. Red proof: injecting a depreciation charge into the cost vector goes red on the equality pin.

## Task D - Residual bases (core)

- ResidualParams extends: the static amount (today's behavior, unchanged) or a percent-of-cost basis (percentPct of the total cost base, at the set year). Both enter once, as the terminal inflow at the residual month, and both reduce the solved requirement - no other touchpoints.
- The percent basis is script-verified at the checkpoint: the terminal inflow equals percentPct x total cost, and k falls as the percent rises.

## Task E - Per-term boundary appraisal (core, then web)

- For each contract, at its end month: the whole project's position - the truncated-flow IRR (the ambiguity discipline travels; null when no sign change yet), the cumulative net (nominal), the NPV at WACC and at target of the truncated flow, and payback-so-far (null when not yet). Labeled "Project position at the end of <contract>" on every surface - the label discipline is pinned (a surface claiming a contract's standalone IRR goes red).
- The combined figures (the existing achieved IRR, NPV, payback, DSCR) stand, computed over the derived horizon.
- DSCR stays whole-project and yearly (per-contract DSCR is out of scope, disclosed).
