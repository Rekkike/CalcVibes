## Chunk 0 — Engine honesty (core/src)

### 0.1 Zero-clamp on the solved payment (F12, F13, F14)

Contract: the solved payment is clamped at zero whenever the raw solve is negative (the entered structure plus residual already covers the target requirement). The engine computes the raw solve, emits the clamp state, and the displayed payment is zero. A negative solved rent must never exist on any surface.

Implementation shape: compute `rawSolvedPayment` exactly as today's `solvedPayment` (both the contracts branch and the legacy branch); then `clampActive = !(tariffCollectionMode || profileMode) && rawSolvedPayment < 0`; `solvedPayment = clampActive ? 0 : rawSolvedPayment`. Add `solvedClamped: boolean` to `ModelResult`.

- PRE (red proof, current main): `paymentAmount = -1832034.3934739418`, `achievedIrr = 0.120000000000001`, `signChanges = 1`. Note the pathology: the manufactured negative rent "closes to target" by construction, so the pre-fix verdict claims the target is met while the structure is nonsense.
- POST: `paymentAmount = 0`, `solvedClamped = true`, `achievedIrr = 0.34533091318844233`, `goalMet = true`, `netGain = 92150140`, `totalCollected = 100000000`, `signChanges = 1`.
- Verdict mapping (consumed by web per chunk 2): `solvedClamped && goalMet` renders as "Target exceeded — the entered structure covers the requirement; no solved rent is needed."

### 0.2 Headline IRR: conditional convention (F15-b)

Contract: the headline achieved IRR is stable and never an arbitrary root.

- If the monthly net flow is conventional (at most one sign change): the headline is the existing monthly-flow `irrAnnual` root. It is unique, and the 144-case solve identity (INV-2, achieved equals target within 1e-9) is preserved untouched.
- If the monthly net flow is non-conventional (two or more sign changes): the monthly root is arbitrary (first-bracket artifact, the −90%-class defect the user observed). The headline falls back to the yearly-aggregated flow: calendar-year sums of the monthly net vector, discounted at annual rates. Add `npvYearly(rateA, flows)` (exponent i+1, end-of-year timing) and `irrYearly(flows)`: all-zero guard, annual-rate grid scan over [-0.9, 6.0] in 96 steps, first sign-change bracket, 300 bisections to |NPV| < 1e-9, returning the annual rate directly (no monthly compounding). If the yearly aggregate is itself non-conventional, the headline is `null` and the ambiguity disclosure carries (no verdict from an ambiguous structure).
- The existing `irrAmbiguous` flag continues to reflect the monthly-flow sign-change count; surfaces disclose non-conventionality alongside the headline.

### 0.5 End-of-case disposition (F14-a, F15-a)

`ResidualParams` gains an explicit `posture: "none" | "set-price" | "book-value"` (optional for schema compatibility; derivation from legacy fields: `mode === "amount" && amount > 0` → `set-price`; `mode === "calculated"` → `book-value`; otherwise `none`). Strict validation:

- `none`: no terminal inflow; amount and year ignored; the book view keeps carrying the value; result is byte-identical to today's amount-0 case (assert in a test).
- `set-price`: finite amount greater than 0 AND integer year of at least 1; empty or zero year is rejected.
- `book-value`: integer year of at least 1; empty or zero year is rejected.

The silent empty-year acceptance (validate.ts: residual year checked only when amount is positive; calculated mode never checks the year) dies: rejection messages must name the field and posture (for example, "The set-price disposition requires a sale year that is an integer of at least 1."). The UI selector presents the three postures by these names; the phantom-influx mislabel ("calculated" read as an accounting statement) dies with the naming.

Assumed-sale disclosure, emitted by the engine alongside the residual result and rendered on every surface that carries the terminal inflow (results, deck, PDF, XLSX): set-price — "Assumes sale at year Y for X"; book-value — "Assumes sale at remaining book value at year Y for X"; none — no disclosure (nothing is assumed; nothing is sold).
