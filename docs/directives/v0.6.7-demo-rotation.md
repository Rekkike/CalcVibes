## Background of record

The user's ruling: replace the two existing demo projects with realistic, well-publicized scenarios — a port infrastructure project of the smaller OPS (shore power) kind in a European continental port, and a land project, newbuild including the ground, at roughly 400 MSEK scale or equivalent — and, while composing them, drive the app properly and surface missing functions for the roadmap.

The grounding, named in the fixture comments: the Rotterdam shore power program (the Port of Rotterdam Authority and Eneco joint venture, shore power at the APMT Maasvlakte II, ECT Delta, and ECT Euromax container terminals, ABB as the installation partner, roughly 35 connection points along about 8 km of quay serving some 32 ships, an EIB loan of 90 M EUR with a public co-funding package of roughly 160 M EUR in total, phased commissioning from 2028); and Mjøstårnet at Brumunddal, Norway (the 18-storey, 85.4 m timber tower by AB Invest and HENT, a totalenterprise construction contract reported at roughly NOK 500 million excluding VAT, hosting the Wood Hotel, offices, apartments, and a restaurant).

Modeling assumptions of record, disclosed in the fixture comments and here: the shore power fixture models one terminal's share of the program and charges the full modeled cost base to the solved per-lift fee (the real program recovers its costs across several terminals with public co-funding, which the app cannot model — finding F4 below); the Mjøstårnet fixture models the hotel and office/restaurant leases with the site acquisition retained and a book-value exit at year 23, and EXCLUDES the apartment sales (the apartments were sold, not leased; the app has no mid-project one-time disposal concept — finding F6), with the remaining owner costs outside the model.

## Task C — the engine honesty fix (core/src/engine.ts, multi-row tariff grid branch only)

The defect: in the multi-row tariff branch of the collections grid, each row's yearly amounts accumulate `perRowCharges` — which are PER-CALL PRICES (weight times unit price), not yearly amounts. The rows are dimensionally wrong by roughly the yearly call volume. Red proof on the delivered main: the port fixture's grid rows sum to 249949.51577988375 (deep-sea) and 124974.75788994187 (feeders) against totalCollected 390293432.14675796 — the deck's inflow donut and the yearly inflow bars under-report by the same amounts, and the GRID-RECON identities fail per year.

The fix (additive, the multi-row branch only): each row's amount for a tariff year is the row's true yearly revenue — `(t.required ?? t.revenue) * (row.weight * (row.lifts[k] ?? 0)) / t.weightedVolume`, guarded to 0 when the weighted volume is 0, where `t` is the tariff year info at grid index `k`. `perRowCharges` itself is UNTOUCHED (its per-call semantics are correct and pinned by followup.test); the single-row path (`rows: []`) is untouched; the contracts, balloon, and residual rows are untouched; the stable/manual/fixed modes take `t.revenue` through the same expression.

Post-fix pins (the port fixture): the deep-sea row sums to 303574464.86520803, the feeder row to 86718967.28154981, and the two together to totalCollected within 1e-6; every year's column sums to that year's inflow within 1e-6.

Task C's test (core/test/collections.test.ts, additive): add a multi-row decompose tariff configuration to GRID-RECON's configuration list (two rows whose lifts keep every grid year's weighted volume positive), asserting the same five identities at 1e-6. This configuration is red on the delivered main and green after the fix — it is the core-level red proof.
