# Project IRR Analyzer — Specification

Living specification. This file is the authority of record for what the application is and does. The changelog at the end grades every change. Current version: **v0.6.13.1** (2026-10-21).

## 1. Purpose and user

A purpose-built investment-appraisal application replacing spreadsheet work (the Invest for Excel class of tooling) for one practitioner: modelling project cost from burn-rate assumptions, solving the repayment stream required to hit a target IRR over a set term, and presenting the result in board-ready form.

## 2. Decisions of record (2026-10-07)

- Primary user: the owner alone. Personal tool; no multi-user, sharing, or permissions machinery.
- Deployment (amended 2026-10-08 by user decision, superseding the desktop-only decision of record): desktop / local remains the primary environment and the v1.0 goal (Tauri wrapper recommended); the application is additionally deployed live via GitHub Pages at https://rekkike.github.io/CalcVibes/ as the walkthrough and delivery vehicle - a client-side static site, no user data hosted, auto-deployed by the GitHub Actions pipeline on every push to main (npm ci, typecheck, the full test battery, build, deploy). The Pages deployment changes no other decision: single-document model and the no-cloud storage layer stand.
- Version 1.0 scope: as complete as possible — the full appraisal suite plus the financing layer.
- Projects: one at a time. Single-document model (one project = one file), like a spreadsheet workbook. No project database, no side-by-side comparison in v1.
- Currency handling: user-selected currency per project; no exchange-rate machinery in v1.
- Quality bar: correctness over the full input space, not only the built-in example. Invariant tests, an explicit numerical policy, and a no-silent-filtering validation contract are part of the definition of correct.

## 3. Product definition

Jobs to be done, in order:

1. Define the project cost plan (burn rate lines, escalations, one-time CAPEX, residual value).
2. Define the repayment structure (term, frequency, grace, escalation, balloon).
3. Set the target IRR and read the solved payment stream, with verified achieved IRR.
4. Stress the result (scenarios, sensitivity tables, WACC benchmark).
5. Model the financing (debt/equity split, drawdown, interest, DSCR).
6. Present it (generated slide deck; exports to PDF and XLSX).

## 4. Calculation engine specification

### 4.1 Time basis

All computation on a monthly-resolution cash-flow vector; annual aggregation for reporting. Monthly rate from annual rate: r_m = (1 + r)^(1/12) - 1. Every metric derives from this one vector; no metric is computed independently of it.
- Project length (delivered at v0.5.3): an explicit top-level projectLengthYears, when present, sets the modeled horizon to exactly N x 12 months; when absent the horizon derives exactly as before and the output is byte-identical (pinned at length 10, every field including mirr). A shorter explicit length surfaces PROJ-LENGTH-EXCEEDED naming every offender - the cost program, the repayment schedule, the collections profile, the tariff grid, the residual, the financing service - with no silent truncation; zero and non-integer values surface PROJ-LENGTH-INVALID.
- Yearly detail grids (delivered at v0.5.3, additive only): the engine emits result.costGrid (cost lines, operating lines, maintenance) and result.collectionsGrid (the profile rows, the payment-stream row, the tariff rows, plus Balloon and Residual rows); the five reconciliation identities hold at 1e-6 across the configurations of record; the byte-identity strip helper of the existing-fields control extends to the two additive fields, the existing-fields identity unchanged in substance.

### 4.2 Cost model

- Cost lines, any number: recurring (annual amount, start year, duration, escalation %/yr) or one-time (amount at a given year).
- Per-year override table (yearOverrides) on any cost line - Invest-for-Excel-style flexibility, recorded at v0.1.0, delivered at v0.5.2, and extended by decision of record to CAPEX lines as a first-class per-year spend profile. Conventions of record (v0.5.2): a CAPEX line with overrides places one lump per year at month 12 x year, its span is the min..max of the override years, the amount/startYear/duration fields are inert while overrides are present, and its lastCostYear contribution is the maximum override year (a profile can move the repayment start); a recurring line's override replaces the computed amount x escalation for that year, spreads monthly like the line's other years, excludes escalation for the overridden year, surfaces an error naming the line and the year when outside the line's span, and never moves the repayment start; override amounts must be positive and finite under the collect-all contract; empty or absent overrides are byte-identical to the unoverridden line. The web year editor carries a spread affordance of record: one total, a first year, and a year count fill equal per-year amounts with the remainder on the final year, so the filled amounts always sum to the entered total exactly.
- Residual value: first-class input, a positive terminal cash flow at a set year. Placement of record (v0.3): the collections side — it participates in NPV(collections) and in the solver and is never netted against costs; zero residual is byte-identical to absent. (Amended at v0.6.12.2, the AUD-7 remediation: a calculated book-value residual dated beyond the derived horizon keeps a sale-year schedule of the remaining book value — a positive sale-year value extends the horizon and enters the vector, and a zero value appears nowhere.)
- Categories for grouping (personnel, infrastructure, CAPEX, other).
- Operating cost lines (v0.3.1 scope of record, user request 2026-10-07): rent, lease, concession, OPEX, and maintenance lines that run alongside collections during the repayment period. A maintenance rule derives a yearly cost from CAPEX (rule of thumb: 0.5 percent of total CAPEX per year, payable from the first year collections begin; changeable to a fixed sum or another percentage). Operation-period costs are costs of the single vector: they raise the solved payment and lower the achieved IRR. Convention of record: operation-period cost lines never move the repayment start — the repayment start is either set explicitly by the user or derived by default from the project-fulfillment lines alone, so these costs run alongside collections rather than deferring them. Delivered at v0.3.1: operating lines (category operating, recurring-line shape) spread monthly like burn lines, excluded from the last-cost-year derivation and the horizon, clipped to the collection window [firstCollectionMonth, lastInflowMonth] (lastInflowMonth = max(lastPaymentMonth, residual month)) — amended at v0.6.12.2, the AUD-4 remediation: in the profile and contracts modes the window is the full actual collection span of the solved model, both ends (the profile span or the contracts' collection span, the residual sale month included), while plain and tariff models keep the repayment-derived window; the engine emits each line nominal span, effective window, and total, and the UI displays them — clipping is never silent, and a line falling entirely outside the window is a surfaced input error naming the line and the true span. The maintenance rule is mode off / percent / fixed, the basis of record resolved to total CAPEX (percent per year of the sum of one-time line totals; superseded at v0.6.12.2, the AUD-3 remediation: the basis is the profile-honoring capex incurred total — the lineIncurred basis the book view uses, honoring per-year overrides, gross of grants, with recurring lines and rent excluded — and the engine emits the basis it used) or a fixed annual sum, running monthly from the collection window's first month with no escalation; the derived line is labeled with its basis and traceable in the cost composition.

### 4.3 Repayment solver — three modes

- Mode A (primary): given target IRR and term, solve the periodic payment P so that NPV(collections) = NPV(costs) at the target rate, honouring frequency, grace, escalation, and balloon.
- Mode B: given P and target IRR, solve the required term (rounded up to whole payment periods).
- Mode C: given P and term, report the achieved IRR (no solving).
- The achieved IRR is always verified numerically (bisection on the NPV function) against the solved structure.
- Delivered at v0.3: Mode B (solveTerm) returns the minimum whole payment count with fractional term years allowed, lastPaymentMonth = start + (n - 1) x spacing, and surfaces infeasibility as null with the discounted shortfall; Mode C evaluates a given payment through the computeModel fixedPayment option; the engine emits goalMet with meets-or-exceeds semantics, superseding the v0.2 UI-side goal check.
- Mode B operating basis (delivered at v0.3.2, closing carried observation 1 of v0.3.1): solveTerm consumes the same operating-augmented cost NPV as Mode A, the operating and maintenance clipping window being [firstCollectionMonth, solved lastPaymentMonth], resolved by fixed-point iteration over the solved payment count — initialize operating-free, solve, recompute the window from the solved count, recompute the basis, and iterate to the fixed point; the reported result costNpv includes the operating contribution. Corrected at v0.3.2.1 (the follow-up pass): the window end is start + (n - 1) x spacing at every iteration, and the discriminating boundary case is pinned (P = 395,400 with 0.5 percent maintenance: 38 payments, window [36, 147], where the v0.3.2 delivery returned 39). Shortfall semantics of record (v0.3.2.1): the feasible-branch shortfall is result.costNpv - P x S(n) on the converged operating-augmented basis (negative or zero at the fixed point); the minimality shortfall is result.costNpv - P x S(n - 1) on the same basis (strictly positive); the infeasible branch keeps the discounted shortfall; S(k) is the discounted slot sum over the payment slots including the schedule escalation. (Amended at v0.6.12.2, the AUD-8 remediation: the term solver takes the full basis — the funding NPV netted, grants and the interest-free loan's subsidy element included; the calculated book-value residual honored; and exactly one solved contract solved on its own slots, its balloon riding the solved end and the evaluated companions netting the requirement — with multiple solved contracts refused under SCE-CONTRACTS and tariff collection modes refused under SCE-TARIFF, both rendered verbatim by the web Repayment section. The operating window keeps the solved-count resolution on the plain path; the contracts basis ends at the last collection of any contract or the sale month of a booked residual.)
- Collection indexation (v0.3.1 scope of record, user request 2026-10-07): CPI-style indexation of collections — collections in year k are scaled by (1 + rate)^(k-1), rule of thumb 2 percent per year when enabled. The arithmetic already exists (the payment escalation parameter). Delivered at v0.3.1: surfaced and labeled Indexation (CPI) percent per year with the 2 percent rule-of-thumb hint; single rate only — per-year overrides deferred by decision of record (2026-10-07); the engine arithmetic is unchanged.
- Repayment start override (v0.3.1 scope of record, user request 2026-10-07): the first collection year is user-settable, including during the cost period — large projects may begin renting out parts of a property before construction completes. Default: derived as the last project-fulfillment cost year plus grace, exactly as today. When collections overlap the cost period, the net vector can change sign more than once; the ambiguity policy of section 4.7 applies, the ambiguity flag is expected rather than exceptional in such structures, and the solver identity still holds at the target rate. Delivered at v0.3.1 with the decision of record (2026-10-07): the explicit start replaces the derivation — the first payment month is firstCollectionYear x 12 for all three modes — and grace must be zero when the override is set (a surfaced input error otherwise); the maintenance window recomputes to the actual collection start.
- Tariff-based collections (v0.3.2 scope of record, user request 2026-10-07): the collection side may be expressed as a tariff decomposition rather than a bare amount. The user defines type rows — each a free-text descriptor (informational only), a weight (a charging coefficient, not a physical mass), and a lift count per year — in a user-determined rows-by-years grid that represents how the costs would actually be recovered (example of record: container lifts over a quay — dry and reefer, loaded, empty, and transhipment — at weights such as 1/2, 0.5/1, and 2/4 for 20- and 40-foot boxes). The weighted volume of a year is the sum over rows of weight times lifts. Two-step flow of record: step one derives the required collection schedule from the target IRR, CAPEX, term, and all other factors (the existing Mode-A solve); step two answers how that requirement is actually recovered, down to currency per lift. Price modes of record, all supported because flexibility is an explicit requirement: (a) solved per year, the default — the unit price of a year is the required collections of that year divided by its weighted volume, and the per-lift charge of a row is its weight times that unit price, so flat volumes inherit the schedule escalation automatically; (b) a stable indexed tariff — one base unit price in first-year terms with an escalation rate, solved so that NPV(tariff collections) = NPV(costs) at the target IRR, a genuine solve variant whose revenue profile is volume-shaped rather than annuity-shaped (a constant nominal tariff is the zero-escalation case); (c) manual per-year prices — user-set, the engine evaluating the resulting profile without solving. The required schedule may alternatively be a user-entered fixed amount per year in place of the solved schedule, the achieved IRR then being reported as the outcome. Conventions of record: mode (a) never changes the solved schedule; mode (b) differs from the annuity by design and its achieved IRR equals the target by construction; residual value remains a separate first-class input and is never decomposed into per-lift prices; a year with zero weighted volume against a positive required collection is a surfaced infeasibility, never a silent zero. The feature composes with the v0.3.1 conventions (the repayment start override and indexation). Delivered at v0.3.2: rows carry an id, a free-text descriptor, a positive weight, and non-negative integer lift counts per grid column; the grid derives from the model span — columns from firstCollectionYear through firstCollectionYear + termYears (termYears + 1 columns; wording corrected at v0.3.2.1: the earlier formulation "through the year containing the last scheduled payment slot" coincides on the template but differs at other payment frequencies — the degenerate final grid cell exists only under the delivered formulation), never hardcoded (eight columns on the template, years 3 through 10; years 2 through 9 under the override); each grid year's revenue spreads evenly over that year's months clipped to at least the first collection month, so the first grid year lands entirely at the first collection month, and the horizon extends to cover the last tariff month; the four price modes are delivered — decompose (the default; the Mode-A solve untouched with schedule byte-identity, the unit price of a year its required collections over its weighted volume, the per-lift charge the row weight times the unit price, and zero weighted volume against a positive required collection a surfaced error naming the year), the stable indexed tariff (a genuine solve variant: one base unit price in first grid-year terms escalated per year, solved so that NPV(tariff collections) = NPV(costs) at the target with the discounted balloon and residual subtracted; achieved IRR equals the target by construction), manual per-year prices (evaluated, not solved), and the fixed required amount per year in place of the solved schedule; in the collection modes (stable, manual, fixed) no annuity is solved — paymentAmount is null and the tariff stream replaces the annuity inflows — while decompose keeps the solved annuity; the residual remains a separate inflow and is never decomposed; the operating and maintenance clipping window ends at the last tariff month in the collection modes only. Scope of record: one tariff layer per project. All six notices of record were resolved by the v0.3.2.1 follow-up pass: decompose clips operating lines to the last payment month and is byte-identical to tariff-off (pinned by JSON equality with the tariff block removed); the per-lift charges are engine-emitted per grid year and per row and the web renders them verbatim; manual mode renders one price input per grid column with engine-level validation (the array length must equal the grid column count, and entries must be finite and non-negative — surfaced errors, never silent zeroing) and a span fallback so the inputs render while the result is invalid during editing; the decomposition table labels columns by model year through the shared header helper; a degenerate decompose cell (zero volume with zero required) emits a null unit price, never NaN; and in the collection modes paymentCount is null with lastPaymentMonth set to the last tariff collection month.
- Repayment collections profile (delivered at v0.5.3): the repayment schedule may be replaced by a per-year collections profile - repayment.collectionsOverrides keyed by model year, each profiled year's amount spread equally across its twelve months. Conventions of record: profile mode bypasses the solved slot stream (paymentAmount and paymentCount null); the modeled horizon is the maximum of the cost span, the profile end, and the residual month; the balloon rides the profile end; the solved-mode balloon guard is inert in profile mode; reporting is honest - npvAtTarget is the reported gap, goalMet follows the standing formula, and the ambiguity flag travels. Conflicts surface as named errors (COL-TARIFF-CONFLICT against a tariff layer, FIN-LEVERAGED-PROFILE against the leveraged solve); profile validation (COL-PROFILE-YEAR, COL-PROFILE-AMOUNT) surfaces under the collect-all contract. Carried item of record: the sensitivity tables and solveTerm must refuse under profile mode with the named error SCE-PROFILE; at v0.5.3 only the web Scenarios section guards (the two payment-solve tables hidden with a visible note in the directive's text) - the core scenario functions and solveTerm are unguarded and return results without meaning in profile mode; the closure is the mandatory opening item of the next directive, and the builder report's claims on this point are corrected by inspection at the v0.5.3 grading. Closed at v0.5.4 (the mandatory opening item of the design pass): the core guards are delivered - both payment-solve tables and solveTerm throw the named error under a non-empty profile, COL-VALID carries its fifth construct with the message asserted, and the web note renders the engine refusal.

### 4.4 Metrics

- NPV at target IRR and NPV at WACC (benchmark discount rate input).
- IRR: bracketed bisection on the NPV function. The bracket is found by a finite grid scan for a sign change over monthly rates [-0.9, 6.0] (96 steps), then bisection to an NPV below 1e-9 or 300 iterations; the monthly root is annualized as (1 + r_m)^12 - 1. An all-zero flow vector yields no IRR. (DEFECT-001 repair of 2026-10-07: the original unbounded bracket [-0.9999, 10] evaluated the NPV at a degenerate rate where denominators underflow, and could collapse to the meaningless value 11^12 - 1.)
- MIRR with explicit finance rate and reinvestment rate.
- Payback (nominal) and discounted payback (at WACC), interpolated, both in years.
- Profitability index: NPV(collections at WACC) / NPV(costs at WACC).
- Total cost, total collected, nominal gain, collection-to-cost ratio.
- Conventions of record, delivered at v0.3: MIRR = (FV of positive net flows at the reinvestment rate to month N / |PV of negative net flows at the finance rate to month 0|)^(12/N) - 1 on the monthly vector, null when either side is empty; profitabilityIndex = npvCollectionsAtWacc / npvCostsAtWacc, null when the cost side is non-positive; discounted payback applies the nominal interpolation rule to the discounted cumulative and equals the nominal payback at WACC 0; goalMet = achievedIrr !== null && achievedIrr >= target/100 - 1e-9.
- Conventions of record, delivered at v0.6.1 (walkthrough remediation, findings F5-F15): the headline achieved IRR is conditional — the monthly-flow root when the monthly net vector has at most one sign change (unique; INV-2 and the golden battery preserved untouched), the yearly-aggregated root when the monthly flow is non-conventional (calendar-year sums of the monthly net vector, end-of-year timing, the same [-0.9, 6.0] grid scan at annual rates with 300 bisections, the annual rate returned directly), and null with the ambiguity disclosure when the yearly aggregate is itself non-conventional; the result emits headlineSource (monthly / yearly / ambiguous), and an arbitrary first-bracket monthly root is disclosed but never displayed as the verdict headline. The solved payment is zero-clamped: a negative raw solve clamps to a zero payment with solvedClamped on the result, so a negative solved rent never exists on any surface. An all-evaluated contract portfolio is valid and reports paymentAmount as null (no solved stream exists; never zero). In contracts mode paymentCount is the count of active payment slots across all contracts within the horizon, not the dormant legacy repayment block. The end-of-case disposition is an explicit three-posture input (none / set-price / book-value; legacy fields derived — amount-positive amount mode maps to set-price, calculated mode maps to book-value, otherwise none) with strict named validation (the silent empty-year acceptance is dead) and the engine-emitted assumed-sale disclosure on every terminal-inflow surface; the none posture is byte-identical to the amount-0 case.

### 4.5 Financing layer (delivered at v0.4)

- Decisions of record (settled 2026-10-08): financing is an overlay — it never changes the project result; financing off (or share zero) is byte-identical to the unfinanced model, pinned. A leveraged-solve toggle, on the user's explicit request, inverts the solve: the collections are solved so that the equity cash flow earns the target IRR ("sometimes we know A and want to find B, sometimes B and want to find A"). It applies to the Mode-A annuity and to the stable-tariff base price; decompose follows the solved schedule byte-identically; manual and fixed modes evaluate only (FIN-LEVERAGED-MODE, a surfaced error); Modes B and C remain project-basis in v0.4. (Superseded at v0.6.12.2 for Mode B: the term solver is full-basis — the funding block netted, the calculated residual honored, exactly one solved contract, per section 4.3; Mode C remains project-basis.)
- Configuration: a project-level debt share with an optional per-line override, an annual debt rate, term and grace in years, a service-start year defaulting to the first collection year, and the amortization type (annuity or equal-principal). Validation surfaces under the collect-all contract: share in [0, 1] at project and line level, debt rate above -100 percent, term at least 1, grace strictly below term, service start within the horizon.
- Funding rule of record: debt funds the fulfillment lines only — recurring burn and one-time CAPEX, the solve's cost basis; operating lines are never funded. Each draw equals the applicable share times the fundable cost at the cost's own month.
- Rate and IDC: the monthly debt rate is r_d = (1 + debtRate/100)^(1/12) - 1. Interest during construction capitalizes over months 1..S: B_m = (B_(m-1) + draw_m) x (1 + r_d), with S the service-start month, serviceStartYear x 12 (default firstCollectionYear x 12). Service runs months S+1 .. S+N with N = termYears x 12; the term includes grace.
- Amortization: grace months are interest-only; principal then amortizes over N - 12 x grace months. Annuity: a constant payment on the running balance. Equal-principal: a fixed principal with declining service.
- Scope lock of record: a fundable cost falling strictly after S is a surfaced validation error in v0.4 — draws during amortization are out of scope.
- DSCR: per model year, year inflows / year service (interest + principal), null in years without service; the minimum is reported with its year, the earliest year on a tie.
- Equity layer: eq(m) = net(m) + draw(m) - service(m) over m = 1..max(project horizon, S+N). Equity outlay = the sum over lines of (1 - share) x fundable cost. Equity NPV at WACC and at target; equity payback by the nominal interpolation rule, with the crossing allowed from an exactly-zero prior cumulative (the first strictly-positive cumulative crossing — pinned by the share-100 case crossing at month 36 from zero outlay); sign changes counted.
- Equity IRR convention of record (2026-10-08): bracketed bisection identical to the project IRR, with the scan restricted to monthly rates [0, 6.0] — the leftmost non-negative root is returned. Enumeration evidence of record (script-verified): the annuity-overlay equity vector alternates monthly (quarterly collections against monthly service — 56 sign changes on the anchor case), and the full-grid first-bracket convention of 4.7 returns a degenerate negative root insensitive to configuration (-0.9855588759 on the anchor case) instead of the economic root (0.2000612172, the pinned value). Annual aggregation was rejected: it breaks the exact identities — the project IRR would read 0.1218985053 instead of exactly 0.12, and the leveraged verification would read 0.1254297972 instead of the exact 0.12. Zero equity outlay (share 100) reports null with the zero-outlay note, never a degenerate root. The ambiguity disclosure renders whenever the sign-change count exceeds one.
- Leveraged solve (closed form, linear in the service present value): P_L = (PV at target of all outflows, operating included - PV at target of draws + PV at target of service - balloon x df - residual x df) / D, with D the esc-weighted slot-sum for the annuity and the volume-weighted discounted-volume sum (the stable-tariff denominator) for the base price. The equity NPV at target is zero and the equity IRR equals the target by construction — pinned on both modes; the leveraged payment is strictly increasing in the debt rate (directional of record).

### 4.6 Scenarios and sensitivity

- Three named assumption sets (base / optimistic / pessimistic): global multipliers on burn rate, escalation shift, IRR-goal shift, term shift.
- One-way sensitivity (tornado) on the required payment: burn rate, target IRR, term, escalation.
- Two-way tables: payment vs. term; payment vs. target IRR; payment vs. balloon.
- User-configurable sensitivity lists (delivered at v0.5.2, user request): the three table axes are project settings - settings.scenarioTargets, settings.scenarioTerms, settings.scenarioBalloons (arrays of numbers; the engine scenario functions already accept arbitrary lists and the engine never receives the settings). Defaults of record when absent: targets 8/12/16 percent, terms 5/7/9 years, balloons 0/1,000,000/2,000,000, rendering today's tables byte-identically. Entry order is preserved as entered (no silent sorting); an explicitly empty array, a negative target, a non-finite entry, a non-positive term, a negative balloon, or a duplicate entry each surface a named error under the collect-all contract.

### 4.7 Numerical and validation policy (of record, 2026-10-07)

- Precision: all computation in IEEE-754 double precision with no intermediate rounding. Rounding occurs only at the display and export boundary.
- Display: values are rounded to the currency's minor unit for presentation; a displayed total is computed from unrounded values, so displayed component rows may differ from a displayed total by at most one minor unit.
- Validation: input errors surface; no silent filtering. A cost line with an empty name, a non-positive amount, or a start year below one is an error to be reported, not a line to be quietly dropped. (Deviation of record: the prototype silently dropped invalid lines; the tool of record reports them. Enforced since the v0.1.1 hardening pass.)
- IRR non-uniqueness: for net-flow vectors with more than one sign change, the IRR may not be unique. The engine counts sign changes across non-zero flows and reports an ambiguity flag alongside the achieved IRR; the UI must warn when the flag is set. The solver returns the first bracket found from the left of the scan grid; this limitation is documented, not hidden. (Amended at v0.6.1: the first-bracket monthly root is disclosed but is no longer the headline on non-conventional monthly flows — the conditional headline convention of section 4.4 governs; the ambiguity flag continues to reflect the monthly-flow sign-change count.) Robustness of record (v0.3.1, script-verified): in every pinned overlapping-collection case the first bracket contains the target monthly rate and the net NPV is strictly positive at every scanned rate below it, so the achieved IRR equals the target and the goal check holds even where the ambiguity flag is set; the disclosure still renders.
- Solver bounds: the IRR search never evaluates the NPV outside monthly rates [-0.9, 6.0].
- Equity IRR bounds (v0.4 convention of record): the equity scan is restricted to monthly rates [0, 6.0] and returns the leftmost non-negative root; a zero equity outlay reports null with the zero-outlay note. See 4.5 for the enumeration evidence.
- Determinism: identical inputs produce identical outputs; the engine contains no randomness, clock, or I/O.
- Directional claims of record (script-verified 2026-10-07): a longer grace strictly raises the solved payment at positive target rates (deferral discounts every collection; at a zero target rate the payment is invariant to grace); a larger balloon strictly lowers the payment; a higher target IRR strictly lowers the cost NPV; a longer term strictly lowers the payment.
- Ambiguity reachability, limitation of record (v0.2.0): under the current input model the ambiguity flag is unreachable — collections begin at or after the last cost month, so the net vector crosses zero at most once. Superseded at v0.3: with residual value constructible, net vectors with multiple sign changes exist (residual 2,000,000 at year 1 yields three sign changes), and the warning is now tested against a real engine result; the component-contract tests remain for the display logic in isolation.

## 5. Application architecture
